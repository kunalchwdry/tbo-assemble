# Demand coverage and potential shortages

The demand coverage page uses the current portfolio, risk setting, budget and selected selling prices. For each row it reproduces the optimizer's demand calculation, including the manual-weight inputs (bookings, searches/conversion, trend, seasonality, signal, sold-out uplift and cancellation), assumed price elasticity, and existing-stock subtraction. Its low/base/high cases are the same as the allocator: subtract existing stock from central price-adjusted demand first, then apply volatility to this remaining demand. This convention is an approximation: it does not model existing-stock sales in each scenario.

Rows with zero suggested purchases use the reference price because their computed zero-quantity price is arbitrary. For each case, covered incremental units are the lesser of the suggested new quantity and incremental demand. Unmet units are the positive remainder. Expected values average these three cases at the planner's assumed 25%, 50%, 25% weights; the high-case shortage is a scenario, not a forecast or confidence bound. Room-nights and seats are summed separately. Allocation is limited by budget, quote expiry relative to the **user-selected review date**, and capacity, not optimized for maximum coverage. No units are reserved or purchased.

Demand, cancellation, elasticity, volatility, and scenario weights are not calibrated; true sell-outs, substitution, booking/refund behavior, and live supplier availability are not included. Sales can be censored by stock. Inventory may be synthetic or user supplied. Set the review date to today and confirm each supplier's current terms before a decision; an old date can include now-expired quotes. Do not interpret a modeled shortage as actual lost sales.
