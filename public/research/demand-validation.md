# Historical demand validation

The workspace includes a browser-only retrospective test and an optional experimental allocation scenario. It does **not** make a forecast production-ready. Use no customer-level or sensitive data. The app cannot establish data provenance, check the truth of reported timestamps, or confirm that a product and unit are representative of TBO's current business.

## CSV contract

Download the template from **Validate demand against history**. One file must cover exactly one product or route (`opportunity_id`) and one unit (`room_nights` or `seats`). One row is a snapshot for one non-overlapping future travel period. All periods must have the same length and the snapshot must have the same lead time before each period starts. Use at most 1,000 rows and 500 KB. Do not mix room-nights and booking records, seats and passengers, destinations, price tiers or different travel products. Do not fill missing days with zeros unless zero is observed.

- `as_of`: snapshot date, strictly before `period_start`. All inputs, including prior-period units and any conversion/cancellation estimate, must have been available on that date. Do not use final revised values, future searches, final cancellations, or statistics fitted using later periods.
- `period_start`, `period_end`: inclusive dates for the future travel period. Periods must not overlap or repeat; one snapshot per period.
- `outcome_available_on`: the date the final outcome first became available, on or after period end. It must include any outcome/label delay. Only outcomes available by the evaluation date are included. Later corrections require their true availability date, not a backdated one.
- `bookings`, `previous_bookings`, `searches`, `previous_searches`, `conversion`, `seasonal`, `signal`, `cancellations`, `sold_out`: historical features for this product, with the same definitions as the planner. Fractions for conversion and cancellations are 0–1; sold_out is 0–100. Numbers must be valid at the snapshot date. Sales inputs must be comparable to the target; where a booking spans multiple room-nights, aggregate into room-nights, not booking records.
- `prior_net_units`: actual net units for a comparable **prior completed period** already known at `as_of`. This is the simple baseline. Its selection must be fixed before evaluation, not chosen after seeing each future outcome.
- `actual_net_units`: final net room-nights or seats sold/consumed for the stated future period. Supply-capped sales understate unconstrained demand. Do not interpret sold-out sales as true demand without availability and censoring information.

The single example line in the template demonstrates formatting, not enough data to test. It is synthetic. Use at least 20 usable training observations and 8 completed holdout observations. If labels overlap or arrive late, additional rows may be required.

## Chronological evaluation

The last 25% of completed snapshots (at least eight) are reserved as a chronological holdout; this choice is fixed before evaluating results. Of earlier rows, only those with outcomes available **strictly before** the first holdout snapshot are eligible for training. The rest are purged. At least 20 eligible training rows are required. Outcomes unavailable as of the selected evaluation date are excluded completely.

A simple blend weight is fit using only training data: among 21 fixed candidate weights from 0% to 100%, select the one minimizing training mean absolute error (MAE). Each prediction uses the planner's current fixed trend, seasonal, signal, sold-out and cancellation multipliers, but varies the booking versus search-conversion weight. The pre-existing planner formula uses a fixed 55% bookings and 45% searches × conversion. Both are compared with the prior-period net-unit baseline on the untouched holdout. We show MAE, weighted absolute percentage error (sum absolute errors divided by sum actual, omitted if the denominator is zero), and the last 12 individual holdout predictions. Smaller is better.

### Rolling-origin replay and descriptive errors

In addition to the fixed-weight holdout, the app replays a separate **rolling-refit procedure** on the same holdout. Before each holdout snapshot it refits the weight from observations with an earlier snapshot and outcomes available **strictly before** that date. Previous holdout outcomes enter later training only after they are available. The first replay has the same earlier training set as the fixed model; later weights can change. The two naive comparisons and default planner are measured on the same holdout rows. The rolling procedure is not the fixed-weight model applied to the plan and is not additional independent data. Its MAE and count of changed weights are displayed, along with the fixed model's mean signed error (prediction minus actual), underprediction and overprediction counts. These are descriptive; no prospective prediction interval or confidence level is implied.

Both the fixed and rolling procedures must beat the user-supplied prior baseline, the default planner and the latest-available-outcome baseline by more than 5% before applying the fixed blend. For each holdout snapshot, the latest-available-outcome baseline is reconstructed from the latest earlier travel period in the same file whose outcome date is strictly before the snapshot; earlier holdout outcomes may be used once available. It does not rely on the supplied prior_net_units field. This is a retrospective comparison with self-reported availability dates, not independent prospective evidence. This is a conservative product rule, not a statistical test: the methods share the same outcomes, results may be selected by repeated trials, and forecast errors can be correlated. The applied fixed weight remains the one trained before the first holdout; the rolling procedure is not applied. The earlier single-split evaluations are unchanged.

This is a retrospective holdout, not a prospective trial. Non-overlap reduces direct period reuse but does not remove temporal correlation, selection bias, changes in price or stock, evolving suppliers, missingness, or geographic shift. Users may choose or revise datasets after seeing results. Model selection, calibration and input provenance need independent evaluation before operational use. The fitted model is not automatically selected; it has no estimated uncertainty interval, validated price elasticity or supplier connection. The existing optimizer and profit calculations remain assumption-driven.


## Optional fitted-blend scenario

After evaluation, the app permits an explicit, temporary transfer of the training-fitted weight to the current product only when both its fixed-blend holdout MAE and the separate rolling-refit MAE are more than 5% lower than the supplied prior baseline, reconstructed latest-available-outcome baseline and default planner on the same periods, there are at least 20 training and 8 holdout observations, ID and unit match, the latest holdout travel period and its outcome are no older than 90 days, and the review date is today UTC. The evaluation date and outcomes must not be in the future relative to that date. The 5% and 90-day thresholds are precautionary rules, not proof of statistical significance, model accuracy or applicability.

When enabled, only the bookings/search blend weight changes in the model; the weights, scenario probabilities, elasticity, risks and other assumptions remain unverified. It flows into allocation, coverage, stress, and the planning API. The optional live attention scenario can also be enabled, but its effect is not validated by the historical holdout. The app cannot verify data provenance, matching travel period, lead time, availability censoring, supplier terms, or whether users selected history after seeing results. It cannot validate the truth of the CSV timestamps. Avoid using these experimental outputs for purchasing decisions without independent validation.

Synthetic example data is generated to demonstrate the workflow and must never be treated as real performance evidence. The fitted weight is not saved with private plan inputs; loading, importing or resetting inventory clears it. A current export labels the selected weight and whether its history was synthetic or user-supplied, but is not a complete reproducible model record. The optional API weight can be supplied directly without holdout proof, so the API labels such results experimental; never trust the request's claimed provenance.

The overview highlights error metrics only when the top selected item has an active fitted scenario; the provenance is synthetic or user-reported. These errors do not validate its price, quantity, risk, supplier availability or expected profit. If an attention scenario is also active, its combined effect was not tested by this replay.


The `prior_net_units` field remains a separate user-supplied baseline and its provenance is not verified. The reconstructed baseline uses only declared completed outcomes from the uploaded series; publication dates, history revisions, sales lost to sell-outs, lead-time alignment with the target plan and the underlying customer population remain unverified. Even this stricter gate cannot establish that the fitted scenario improves real TBO decisions.
